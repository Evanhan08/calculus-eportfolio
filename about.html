Calculus in IT and Web Development

Even though Calculus is very math-heavy, a lot of its core concepts actually connect to technology. As a Web Development student, I realized that what we learned about rates of change, performance, and efficiency applies directly to programming and tech situations.

  1. Rules of Differentiation & Changing Values: Differentiation is basically about tracking how one value changes relative to another. In coding, variables are always changing while a program runs. Understanding this is super useful when working with dynamic calculations, simulations, or interactive web features like custom calculators and charts.
  2. Derivatives & System Performance: Derivatives let you analyze rates of change. In IT, this comes into play when looking at system metrics—like tracking how response times or server usage change as traffic increases. It gives you a mathematical way to look at how a system scales.
  3. Optimization & Finding Efficient Solutions: Optimization is all about finding the maximum or minimum value within certain limits. In web dev, we do this kind of thinking all the time—trying to cut down page load times, minimize resource usage, or make a site run as smoothly as possible.

In the end, it made me realize that being good at math isn't just about getting the right answer on paper, but also being able to break it down and share it with people who are struggling.

Final Reflection

My experience in 2CALC-IT was honestly way better than I expected. Coming into the class, I figured Calculus would be pretty tough with all the heavy steps and formulas. But as we got further into the semester, I actually started enjoying it. I realized that doing math isn't just about memorizing steps, but really understanding how the solution works.

The three main topics we covered were Rules of Differentiation, Applications of Derivatives, and Optimization. Differentiation showed me how to find derivatives using different rules, while applications of derivatives helped me see how math can analyze changing situations. Optimization was all about finding max or min values. Studying these made me see that Calculus is more than just blindly plugging numbers into equations—you actually have to understand the problem first to know which method to use.

My favorite part of the course was definitely making tutorial videos for my classmates. I wanted to help them out by explaining things in my own style, which was a bit simpler and more direct than standard lecture slides. Making those videos didn't just help my classmates—it proved to me that everyone processes math differently and that you don't truly get a topic until you can explain it to someone else.

As a Web Development student, I can also see how these concepts tie into tech. Rates of change and optimization connect really well with how we think about system efficiency, data changes, and solving technical problems. Even if I'm not writing out derivatives in every coding project, that problem-solving logic is still super useful.

Overall, 2CALC-IT helped me appreciate math a lot more. It taught me that getting the right answer is only half the battle—the other half is knowing why it works and being able to share that knowledge. Moving forward, I want to keep building on these problem-solving skills and use what I learned in my future web projects.
